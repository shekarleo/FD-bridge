The problem we are solving

A customer needs cash for a few weeks or months. Their savings sit in a fixed deposit, so they open the early closure screen. Closing the FD looks like the only way to get the money.

Closing early costs twice. First, the bank pays the lower card rate for the period the FD actually ran, for example 6.25% instead of 6.5%. Then it deducts a penalty of up to 1%. On a ₹10 lakh FD closed after one year, that is a one-time loss of about ₹12,500. On top of that, the money stops earning and the locked-in rate is gone.

Two other routes exist, and the closure screen does not show them:

  • Loan against the FD. The bank lends up to 90% of the FD at 2% above the FD rate. Say ₹9 lakh at 8.5% against a 6.5% FD. The loan interest is ₹76,500 a year, but the FD still earns ₹58,500 on the same money. The net cost is the spread: ₹18,000 a year, or ₹1,500 a month. If the loan works like an overdraft, interest is charged only on what the customer actually draws.
  • Personal loan. The FD is untouched, but the rate is much higher and a processing fee applies.

Nobody can compare these costs in their head, in a hurry, and tax makes it harder. So most people take the path on the screen and pay the penalty.

What we want to change

Show the costs in rupees, side by side, on the closure screen. Show which is cheapest, and the number of days after which the answer flips. The customer decides in seconds, and can see the numbers are fair.

What the customer sees

The screen is built for someone who does not think in interest rates. It asks two questions and gives one answer.

  • Two inputs. How much, and for how long.
  • One answer. The cheapest way, and how many rupees it saves.
  • Three numbers. What each option costs, in plain words, with no rates or formulas.
  • One sentence on timing. Up to how many days borrowing is cheaper.
  • The working, one tap away. Costs line by line, the chart, the tax band and the bank assumptions sit under "See how this is worked out". That is also the view for the business review.

Why the bank gains too

  • The deposit stays on the books until maturity instead of leaving today.
  • The bank earns interest on a loan that is backed by the FD itself, so credit risk is low.
  • The customer finishes the journey with us, not with a personal loan app.
  • A customer who is shown the honest answer, even when it is "close the FD", is more likely to trust the bank next time.

What the customer should know beyond the rupees

  • Tax. FD interest is taxed at the slab rate. Interest on a personal-use loan gets no tax break. Closing early gives up taxable interest, so part of that loss comes back as lower tax. This is why the calculator asks for the slab, and why the loan wins for fewer days at a higher slab. Loan interest may be deductible when the money is used for business. A switch under Bank assumptions covers that.
  • The FD is pledged. It cannot be touched until the loan is repaid.
  • Repay before maturity. If the loan is still open when the FD matures, the bank recovers what is owed from the FD. That works like an early closure. The calculator limits the days to the FD's remaining term.
  • Discipline. A loan forces repayment, so the savings are rebuilt. A broken FD is often never rebuilt. The calculator does not put a rupee value on this. For some customers it decides the choice.

How the cost is worked out

Every figure is the customer's cost in rupees over the days they need the money, after tax. Interest is simple, on a 365-day year.

Loan against FD = Amount × (FD rate + spread) × Days ÷ 365 + fee Close FD early = Lower-rate loss + Lost interest + Re-booking loss − Tax saved Lower-rate loss = Amount closed × (card-rate gap + penalty) × Months run ÷ 12 Lost interest = Amount × FD rate × Days ÷ 365 Re-booking loss = Amount re-invested × Rate fall × Years left Tax saved = Tax slab × (the three items above) Personal loan = Amount × Loan rate × Days ÷ 365 + fee Cost per year = Cost ÷ (Amount × Days ÷ 365)

Loan interest and fees get no tax relief, unless the loan is for business use. Closing the FD early is compared over the same period, up to the FD's original maturity.

While a loan against the FD runs, the FD keeps earning on the same money. That interest covers most of the loan interest, so the real extra is the spread.

A loan against the FD costs more per day than closing early. Closing early has a one-time loss. So the loan wins for short needs, and closing early wins once the days pile up. A higher tax slab brings that crossover earlier. The chart on the calculator tab shows where it falls.
